Marketing Measurement with Google Meridian

I built a Bayesian Marketing Mix Modeling workshop around a fictional e-commerce business to explore a practical question: how do we estimate marketing impact and decide where to spend the next dollar? The project was presented in a graduate-student conversation at Universidad Galileo and includes a reproducible Python notebook, visual diagnostics, and a budget scenario simulator.

The problem

Last-click attribution assigns credit to the final touchpoint, but it cannot tell us what would have happened without advertising. For a business spending across multiple channels, seasonality, promotions, and changing consumer demand make the real impact hard to isolate. This project demonstrates how a probabilistic MMM can help frame the problem, estimate response curves, and support budget discussions—with explicit uncertainty and causal limitations.

What I built

01 · Synthetic business data

Generated a weekly revenue series with Google Search, Meta, and YouTube advertising activity, alongside promotional and demand-related inputs. The modeled KPI is revenue (USD), not conversion counts.

02 · Bayesian media-response model

Used Meridian to illustrate carryover (adstock), diminishing returns (Hill saturation), and posterior uncertainty. The workshop includes MCMC convergence checks and model-fit visualizations.

03 · Spend-response analysis

Built channel-level response curves showing how the model's estimated incremental revenue changes across spend levels, including uncertainty bands.

04 · Interactive budget scenarios

Added budget sliders for testing changes across Search, Meta, and YouTube. The notebook compares historical spend with a proposed scenario and calculates modeled incremental revenue changes.

What the work demonstrates—and what I'd validate next

Key takeaways

  • Prediction accuracy and causal marketing measurement are different objectives.
  • Bayesian priors and posterior distributions make some assumptions and uncertainty explicit, but do not automatically solve confounding.
  • Response curves connect statistical modeling to a business decision: where incremental spend might be most productive.
  • A budget proposal needs to account for additional spend, profit margins, and operational constraints.

Production-readiness checks

  • Out-of-time predictive evaluation, residual inspection, and posterior predictive checks.
  • Sampling diagnostics: R-hat, effective sample size, and divergences.
  • Prior sensitivity, credible control variables, and media variation/collinearity.
  • Calibration against independent geo-lift or conversion-lift experiments where available.
